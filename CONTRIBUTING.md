# Contributing to EXPANSE

Thank you for your interest in EXPANSE. Contributions are welcome from researchers, network engineers, radio enthusiasts, developers, infrastructure operators, and technical writers.

## Before submitting

1. Check whether the topic already exists in the research notes or issue tracker.
2. State the problem and the question your contribution addresses.
3. Separate documented facts, experimental observations, assumptions, and hypotheses.
4. Prefer primary sources: specifications, RFCs, project documentation, source code, and reproducible measurements.
5. Record versions, configuration, topology, and test conditions when reporting technical behaviour.

## Evidence labels

Use one of the following labels where relevant:

- **Confirmed** — supported by authoritative documentation or reproducible evidence.
- **Experimental** — observed under stated test conditions; not necessarily generalizable.
- **Hypothesis** — proposed explanation or design that requires validation.
- **Unverified** — evidence is currently insufficient.
- **Refuted** — contradicted by reliable evidence or repeatable tests.

Do not present a hypothesis as an established capability.

## Research notes

A useful research note should include:

- Title and date.
- Research question.
- Scope and assumptions.
- Technology and version details.
- Sources, preferably primary.
- Findings and evidence label.
- Limitations, risks, and unresolved questions.
- Suggested next experiment or review.

## Experiments

An experiment report should describe:

- Objective and success criteria.
- Hardware and software.
- Network topology and transport types.
- Configuration and reproducible steps.
- Failure conditions introduced.
- Measurements and raw observations.
- Results, limitations, and interpretation.

Avoid publishing sensitive operational details, credentials, private keys, personal data, or information that could expose a vulnerable network or its participants.

## Pull requests

- Keep changes focused and reasonably small.
- Explain why the change is needed.
- Link related issues or research notes when available.
- Include sources for factual technical claims.
- Identify unverified statements explicitly.
- Do not add dependencies or impose a technology choice without explaining the trade-offs.

## Language

The project may accept contributions in Ukrainian and English. The canonical language for a document should be stated where ambiguity is possible. Translations should preserve technical meaning and evidence labels; they must not silently strengthen claims.

## Licensing

The repository licence has not yet been selected. By submitting a contribution, do not assume that it is automatically released under a particular open-source licence. Licence and reuse terms will be clarified in a separate project decision.
